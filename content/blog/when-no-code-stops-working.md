No-code stops working when you spend more effort on workarounds than on your business. The clearest signs are stacked tools held together by manual steps, costs that rise faster than your revenue, hard limits on data or speed, and features you cannot build at all. If two or three of these sound familiar, plan a move.

## The seven signs

### 1. You need workarounds for basic things
A simple rule needs five connected tools, a spreadsheet and a person to check it. The process works only because someone remembers to do a step.

### 2. Costs grow faster than value
Per-user, per-record or per-task pricing climbs as you grow, and the monthly bill starts to look like a salary.

### 3. You hit data limits
Row limits, slow queries, and trouble when you need reports across many records. Spreadsheets and visual databases are great until they are not.

### 4. Performance is slipping
Pages or apps get slow, and you cannot fix the cause because you do not control the code. See [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-plain-english).

### 5. You cannot build the feature you need
Custom pricing logic, role-based access, a quote builder, a specific integration. The platform simply does not allow it.

### 6. Security and access control worry you
You need fine-grained permissions, audit trails or data isolation that the tool cannot provide.

### 7. You are locked in
You cannot export your data cleanly or move your workflows, so every month you stay raises the cost of leaving.

## A quick self-check

| If this is true… | It suggests |
|---|---|
| Fewer than 2 signs | Stay and keep it tidy |
| 2 to 3 signs | Plan a migration of the most painful part |
| 4 or more | Start a rebuild project |

## What to do

1. **List the workarounds** and what each costs in time.
2. **Move the most painful part first,** not everything at once.
3. **Keep the no-code tool** for what it does well.
4. **Export your data** regularly so you are never trapped.
5. **Write down the rules** your process follows, because that is the specification for the custom build.

## FAQ

### How do I know if I have outgrown no-code?
When workarounds, rising costs, data or speed limits, or missing features take more effort than the tool saves.

### Should I rebuild everything at once?
Usually no. Replace the most painful part first and keep tools that still work well.

### Is no-code bad?
No. It is excellent for validating ideas and for simple needs. It just has limits.

### Can I keep using no-code alongside custom software?
Yes. Many businesses keep no-code for simple tasks and build custom software for core workflows.
