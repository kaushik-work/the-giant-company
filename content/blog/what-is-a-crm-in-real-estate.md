A CRM in real estate is software that records every buyer enquiry, where it came from, who is following it up and what happens next, from first call to site visit to booking. It matters because property sales depend on fast follow-up, and leads arrive from many sources that no single person can track in their head.

## What a real estate CRM tracks

- **Leads and their source:** portals, website, ads, referrals, walk-ins, WhatsApp.
- **Buyer details and requirements:** budget, location, type of property, timeline.
- **Interactions:** calls, messages, notes and visits.
- **Site visits:** scheduled, completed and feedback.
- **Stages:** enquiry, qualified, site visit, negotiation, booking, lost.
- **Owner and next action** for every lead.

## Why it matters

1. **Speed:** the first developer or agent to respond often gets the visit. See [how fast to reply to a lead](/blog/how-fast-reply-to-a-lead).
2. **No lost leads:** staff leave and phones change; the CRM keeps the history.
3. **Real marketing numbers:** you can see which portal or campaign produces bookings, not just enquiries.
4. **Better follow-up:** reminders and stages keep deals moving.

## What to set up first

1. **Lead sources** and how each enters the CRM.
2. **Stages** that match how you actually sell.
3. **Assignment rules** by project or location.
4. **Site-visit reminders** for buyers and staff.
5. **A weekly report** of leads, visits and bookings by source.

## FAQ

### What does CRM stand for in real estate?
Customer relationship management: software to manage buyer enquiries, follow-ups and sales.

### Why do real estate agents use a CRM?
To capture leads from many sources, follow up quickly, schedule site visits and see which sources produce bookings.

### Is a spreadsheet enough?
For very few leads, maybe. Once several people follow up, a CRM prevents missed leads.

### What is the first thing to set up?
Lead sources, stages and assignment rules, then reminders for site visits.
