The four commonly described types of CRM are operational, analytical, collaborative and strategic. Operational CRM runs day-to-day sales, marketing and service. Analytical CRM studies customer data. Collaborative CRM shares customer information across teams and channels. Strategic CRM focuses on long-term customer relationships. Most small businesses mainly need operational CRM.

Different authors group CRM slightly differently, but these labels are the most common.

## The four types

| Type | What it does | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational** | Automates sales, marketing and service tasks | Lead capture, pipeline, follow-up reminders, quotes |
| **Analytical** | Analyses customer data to find patterns | Conversion by source, customer segments, churn |
| **Collaborative** | Shares customer information between teams and channels | Sales, support and accounts see the same history |
| **Strategic** | Builds long-term customer relationships and value | Loyalty, retention and account planning |

## Which one do you need?

- **Just starting with a sales team:** operational CRM. You need leads, owners, stages and reminders.
- **Growing and wanting to know what works:** add analytical features: source and conversion reports.
- **Several teams touching the customer:** collaborative features so nobody asks the customer to repeat themselves.
- **Mature accounts and repeat business:** strategic work on retention and account management.

Most products blend these types. What matters is whether the tool fits your process.

## FAQ

### What are the 4 types of CRM?
Operational, analytical, collaborative and strategic.

### Which type of CRM is most common?
Operational CRM, which handles day-to-day sales, marketing and service tasks.

### Is a CRM only for sales?
No. It also supports marketing, service and account management, though sales is the usual starting point.

### Do small businesses need analytical CRM?
Basic reports on lead sources and conversion are useful early. Advanced analytics can wait.
