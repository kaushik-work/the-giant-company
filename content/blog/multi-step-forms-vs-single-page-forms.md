Use a single-page form when you need only two or three fields. Use a multi-step form when you need several details and want to qualify leads, because starting with easy questions can make a long ask feel lighter. Evidence on conversion is mixed, so test on your own audience.

## How they differ

A **single-page form** shows every field at once. It is quick to scan and quick to submit.

A **multi-step form** breaks the questions into short steps with a progress indicator. Each step feels small, and you can ask choice-based questions first.

## When multi-step helps

- You need five or more pieces of information.
- You want to qualify (service needed, industry, budget, timeline) before asking for contact details.
- Choices are easier as buttons than as typed text.
- Visitors may be unsure what they want, and the first step helps them decide.

## When single-page is better

- You need only name, phone and a message.
- Visitors are ready to act, for example a quote request from a pricing page.
- Mobile speed matters and every extra tap costs you.

## Design rules for multi-step forms

1. **Start with an easy, interesting question,** not contact details.
2. **Show progress,** such as step dots.
3. **Keep each step short.**
4. **Let people go back** without losing answers.
5. **Save as you go** if you can, so a partial answer is still useful.
6. **Ask for contact details last,** once the visitor has invested a little.
7. **Validate on the step,** not after the final submit.

## What we did on our own site

Our contact page is a four-step flow: what you need, your business, a rough budget, then name, phone and work email. We chose steps because we wanted to qualify enquiries and because choosing from cards is easier than writing a paragraph. We pass the page a visitor came from into the flow, so we know which article or service sent the lead.

We do not yet have enough data to claim it converts better than a single form, and we would suggest the same caution to anyone. Run your own test, compare completion and lead quality, and keep what works.

## FAQ

### Do multi-step forms increase conversions?
Sometimes. Results vary by audience and length, so test rather than assume.

### How many steps is too many?
Keep it to three or four short steps. If you need more, ask whether every question is necessary.

### Should the contact details come first or last?
Usually last. Asking for them after easy questions reduces the feeling of commitment.

### Is a multi-step form good for mobile?
Yes, if each step is short and tap-friendly.
