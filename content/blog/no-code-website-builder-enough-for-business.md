A no-code website builder is enough for many small businesses: a brochure site, a portfolio, a simple shop or a landing page. It stops being enough when you need custom workflows, deep integrations, strict performance control or a site that behaves like an application. Start with the simplest tool that meets your goals, and know the warning signs that you have outgrown it.

We build custom websites, and we still say this plainly: if a builder does the job, use it.

## What no-code builders do well

- **Speed.** You can launch in days, with no developer.
- **Low upfront cost.** Hosting, templates and editing are bundled.
- **Easy editing.** Non-technical staff can change text and images.
- **Good enough SEO basics.** Titles, descriptions and sitemaps are usually available.

## Where they struggle

| Area | Typical limit |
|---|---|
| **Custom logic** | Quote builders, calculators, multi-step flows, role-based dashboards |
| **Integrations** | Limited to what the platform or its plugins offer |
| **Performance** | Heavy page weight you cannot always fix |
| **Control over SEO structure** | URL patterns, structured data and generated pages can be restricted |
| **Ownership and portability** | You may not be able to take your site and move it |
| **Cost at scale** | Plans and add-ons grow with traffic, pages and features |

## A quick decision guide

**Use a builder if:**
- You need a simple site fast.
- You will edit it yourself.
- Your needs are common: pages, a form, a blog, a basic shop.

**Go custom if:**
- Your site is part of your operations, for example quotes, listings or customer accounts.
- You need to connect it deeply to a CRM, payments or WhatsApp.
- You want full control of speed, structure and data.
- You are generating many pages from data.

## Before you commit to a builder

1. **Can you export your content and data?**
2. **What do the paid plans cost at the size you expect in two years?**
3. **Can you add the integrations you need?**
4. **How does it score for real-user speed?** See [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-plain-english).
5. **Can you set clean URLs, titles, canonicals and structured data?**

## FAQ

### Is a no-code website builder good enough?
For simple sites, yes. For sites that need custom logic, deep integrations or tight control, you will probably outgrow it.

### Are no-code websites bad for SEO?
Not inherently. Many allow titles, descriptions and sitemaps, but you may have less control over structure and speed.

### How much does a no-code website cost?
Plans vary and can grow with features and traffic. Compare the total cost over two years, not just the first month.

### Can I move from a builder to a custom site later?
Yes, though you should plan the migration so you keep URLs and rankings.
