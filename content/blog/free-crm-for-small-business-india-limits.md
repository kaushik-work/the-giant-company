Free CRM plans are real and useful for very small teams: they usually give you contact management, a basic pipeline and some email logging. The limits show up as you grow: a cap on users, restricted automation and reports, limited storage or integrations, and branding. Use one to learn your process, and plan the move before the limits slow you down.

Free plans change often, so check each vendor's current terms.

## What free plans usually include

- Contacts, companies and a basic deal pipeline.
- Notes, tasks and simple reminders.
- Some email integration and a mobile app.

## Limits to look for

| Limit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **User cap** | You outgrow it when the team grows |
| **Automation** | Assignment, sequences and workflows may be paid |
| **Reports and dashboards** | Source and conversion reports may be restricted |
| **Custom fields and stages** | You may not model your process |
| **Integrations and API** | WhatsApp and website forms may need a paid tier |
| **Storage and records** | Caps on contacts or files |
| **Support** | Often community only |
| **Branding** | Vendor branding on forms and emails |

## Who free suits

A solo owner or a team of two or three with a simple process and few leads. It is a good way to build the habit of logging everything.

## When to move on

When you hit a limit that costs you time, when people start keeping side spreadsheets, or when you need WhatsApp, quotes or automation the free tier lacks.

## FAQ

### Is there a free CRM for small businesses?
Yes, several vendors offer free plans with limits. Check their current terms.

### What is the catch with free CRMs?
Limits on users, automation, reports, storage and integrations, and sometimes vendor branding.

### Can I switch from a free CRM later?
Usually, if you can export your data. Check export before you start.

### Is a spreadsheet better than a free CRM?
A CRM gives you reminders, ownership and history. A spreadsheet works for a handful of leads.
