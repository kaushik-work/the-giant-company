A customer support chatbot works best on repeat, factual questions with a clear answer, such as order status, opening hours, how a process works or where to find a document. Anything involving money disputes, complaints, emotion or unusual cases should go to a person, quickly and with context.

## What to automate first

Start by reading a month of real support messages and sorting them. You will usually find that a small number of questions make up most of the volume.

| Good for a chatbot | Better for a person |
|---|---|
| "Where is my order or booking?" | Refund or compensation disputes |
| "What are your hours or locations?" | Upset or distressed customers |
| "How do I change my details?" | Complex, multi-step problems |
| "What documents do I need?" | Anything legally sensitive |
| Questions answered in your FAQ | Exceptions to policy |

## Design the hand-off before the bot

The failure that customers remember is being trapped. Design the exit first:

- Offer a clear "talk to a person" option at any point.
- Trigger a hand-off automatically on frustration signals (repeated rephrasing, strong language) or low confidence.
- Pass the conversation summary to the agent so the customer does not repeat themselves.
- Say honestly what hours a human is available.

## Keep it accurate

The bot should answer from your own content, not from general knowledge. If it does not know, it should say so and offer a hand-off, rather than guess. Read real conversations every week and add the missing answers.

## Measure what matters

- **Resolution without a human**, but only counting cases where the customer did not come back.
- **Hand-off rate and reason**, to find gaps in your content.
- **Customer feedback** on the conversation, even a simple thumbs up or down.

## FAQ

### Can an AI chatbot replace customer support staff?
It can take over repeat, factual questions, which frees staff for harder cases. Most businesses still need people for complaints, exceptions and anything sensitive.

### How do I stop a support chatbot from making things up?
Limit it to your own approved content, instruct it to say when it does not know, and review real conversations regularly.

### Should I tell customers they are talking to a bot?
Yes. Being clear builds trust and avoids frustration when the bot reaches its limits.

### What if the bot cannot solve the problem?
Hand off to a person with the conversation summary. A smooth hand-off matters more than the bot's accuracy on easy questions.
