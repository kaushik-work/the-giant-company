You may not need an SEO expert. Google says small local businesses can often do much of the work themselves by following its SEO starter guide. If you do hire one, ask for examples and a clear plan, give only read-only Search Console access at first, and walk away from anyone who guarantees a number-one ranking.

This post summarises Google's own guidance, which we agree with.

## When you can do it yourself

Many basics need no specialist: a clear site, unique titles and descriptions, useful content, mobile-friendly pages, a sitemap and a [Search Console](/blog/google-search-console-setup-guide) property. Learning the basics also helps you spot bad advice. Ads, Google notes, have no effect on organic rankings.

## When to hire

The best time is during a redesign or before launching a new site, because the earlier an SEO is involved, the better. A good SEO can also improve an existing site.

## Questions to ask

1. Can you show examples of past work and success stories?
2. Do you follow Google's Search Essentials?
3. What results should I expect, in what timeframe, and how will we measure them?
4. What is your experience in my industry and market?
5. Will you explain every change you make, and why?
6. Can you reference official documentation for your recommendations?

Ask past clients whether the work was useful and easy to work with.

## Red flags

- **Guaranteed rankings.** No one can guarantee a number-one position, and claims of a special relationship with Google or "priority submit" are false.
- **Secret methods.** You are responsible for what a company does on your site. Anyone with access should explain every change.
- **Buying links or link schemes,** or submitting your site to thousands of search engines.
- **Unsolicited emails** offering SEO services.
- **Tools said to be "approved by Google".** Google does not endorse third-party SEO tools, and those tools do not have Google's internal ranking data.

## Giving access safely

For an audit, grant **read-only** Search Console access. Do not give write access at the start.

## "AI SEO" claims

If someone sells AEO or GEO (optimising for AI answers), check that their advice matches Google's own guidance on generative AI in Search, and ask for evidence rather than promises.

## FAQ

### Do I need an SEO expert for my small business?
Often not at first. Google says small local businesses can do much of the work themselves using its starter guide.

### Can anyone guarantee a number one ranking?
No. Be wary of anyone who does.

### What access should I give an SEO consultant?
Read-only Search Console access for an audit. Avoid write access at the start.

### How long does SEO take?
Google does not give timeframes. Ask any SEO what results to expect, when, and how they will be measured.
